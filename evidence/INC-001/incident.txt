INC-001 - I/O Saturation
Evidence: Incident
Environment: Linux / WSL2

=== Controlled Workload ===

Command used to generate write activity:

dd if=/dev/zero of=io-test-file bs=1M count=4096 conv=fdatasync

Observed during the controlled workload:

Approximately 4.1 GB copied before interruption.
Elapsed time: approximately 2982.84 seconds.
Observed throughput: approximately 1.4 MB/s.


=== Device-Level I/O ===

Command:

iostat -xz 1 5

Observed samples during the investigation:

I/O wait reached approximately: 46.15%
Device: /dev/sdd
Device utilization reached approximately: 95.20%

Increased write latency and queue activity were observed during
the controlled workload.

Earlier samples also included:

iowait: 22.70%
r_await: 70.86 ms
w_await: 201.76 ms
aqu-sz: 2.80
util: 55.96%

Another sample included:

iowait: 30.63%
w_await: 57.17 ms
f_await: 86.53 ms
aqu-sz: 2.50
util: 80.00%


=== Process-Level I/O ===

A smaller controlled write test was later executed:

dd if=/dev/zero of=io-test-file bs=1M count=256 conv=fdatasync

Command used to observe process I/O:

pidstat -d 1

Observed:

Process: dd
PID: 33582
Write activity: approximately 262144 kB/s


=== Block Device ===

Command:

lsblk

Observed:

/dev/sdd was associated with the Linux root filesystem.

The Docker data directory was also located on the root filesystem.


=== Evidence Correlation ===

During the controlled workload, increased device-level I/O activity
was observed while the dd process was generating intensive writes.

pidstat provided process-level evidence identifying dd as the
controlled writer.
